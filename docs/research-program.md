# Research Program

KARADAVI's public research exists to make the foundations of an open, continuously evolving knowledge institution inspectable and testable.

A research question should produce evidence, a failed hypothesis, a specification decision or a clearly documented reason not to proceed. Product ambitions are not evidence that a mechanism works.

## Track A — Identity and canonical entities

**Question:** How should a system identify and represent an entity when sources use aliases, incomplete identifiers or conflicting descriptions?

Research includes entity resolution, duplicate detection, canonical identifiers, type aliases, ambiguity and revision of identity decisions.

Success means ambiguity remains visible instead of being silently collapsed, and identity decisions can be inspected and revised.

## Track B — Evidence and claims

**Question:** How should observations become evidence for claims, and how should claims change as evidence changes?

Research includes claim lifecycle, supporting and contradicting evidence, temporal context, retraction and correction.

Success means a consuming system can inspect the path from a claim back to its supporting or conflicting material.

## Track C — Provenance and transformation

**Question:** How can provenance survive retrieval, extraction, normalization, entity resolution, interpretation, drafting and publication?

Success means transformations do not erase origin, and a consumer can distinguish source material from machine-derived and human-approved representations.

## Track D — Authority and context

**Question:** How should authority depend on claim type, domain, jurisdiction, time and context?

Success means the model can distinguish “authoritative for this question” from “universally authoritative.”

## Track E — Uncertainty and conflict

**Question:** How should uncertainty propagate when claims depend on incomplete observations or other uncertain claims? How should conflicting sources remain represented?

Success means unknown, ambiguous, stale, incomplete, disputed and contradicted states remain explicit rather than disappearing during aggregation.

## Track F — Trust and evaluation

**Question:** What useful machine-readable evaluation can be produced without reducing the reasoning to an unexplained scalar?

Success means outputs remain inspectable and reproducible from their inputs, methods, evidence and limitations.

## Track G — Knowledge change and stewardship

**Question:** How should a knowledge institution detect, propose, review and record changes over time?

Research includes source change detection, stale-knowledge signals, new-entity discovery, missing relationships, reconciliation and revision history.

Success means changes can be proposed and traced without granting autonomous systems publication authority.

## Research standard

Every substantial experiment should document:

- question;
- hypothesis;
- inputs and source selection;
- method;
- observations;
- limitations and uncertainty;
- negative or conflicting results;
- conclusion; and
- next experiment.

Negative results are first-class research outputs. A failed hypothesis can be more valuable than a convenient positive result when it narrows the design space.

## Editorial boundary

Research and automation may discover, extract, compare, normalize and draft. They do not publish. Human editorial approval remains a separate and mandatory decision.
