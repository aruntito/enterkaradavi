# Research Program

KARADAVI's public work is organized around a small set of questions. Each question should eventually produce either evidence, a failed hypothesis, a specification decision, or a clearly documented reason not to proceed.

## Track A — Identity

**Question:** How should a machine represent an entity when sources disagree about identity?

Success means ambiguity remains visible instead of being silently collapsed.

## Track B — Evidence

**Question:** How should observations become evidence for claims?

Success means a consuming system can inspect the path from a claim back to its supporting material.

## Track C — Authority

**Question:** How should authority depend on claim, domain, jurisdiction, time, and context?

Success means the model can distinguish “authoritative for this question” from “universally authoritative.”

## Track D — Uncertainty

**Question:** How should uncertainty propagate when claims depend on uncertain observations or other derived claims?

Success means uncertainty is preserved instead of disappearing during aggregation.

## Track E — Trust

**Question:** What useful machine-readable trust state can be produced without reducing the reasoning to an unexplained scalar?

Success means the output remains inspectable and reproducible from its inputs.

## Research standard

Every substantial experiment should document:

- question;
- hypothesis;
- inputs;
- method;
- observations;
- limitations;
- conclusion; and
- next experiment.

Negative results are first-class research outputs. A failed hypothesis can be more valuable than a convenient positive result when it narrows the design space.
